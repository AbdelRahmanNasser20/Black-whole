# R6 — Legal / ToS Pass Per Source

**Scope:** GovDeals, Public Surplus, Municibid, MiBid (Michigan), GovPlanet, Purple Wave, GSA Auctions (gsaauctions.gov + api.gsa.gov), iBid Illinois, HiBid, Bid4Assets, PropertyRoom, BidSpotter.

**Method:** fetched `robots.txt` for every host (curl, polite UA, single request per host); located each site's Terms of Service / Terms of Use / User Agreement page via search + direct fetch; quoted any clause governing scraping, automated access, or data reuse; checked for a sanctioned official API/RSS/export/partner channel. Two sites (GovDeals, Bid4Assets) run Akamai Bot Manager + a client-rendered Angular SPA that returns **HTTP 403 to every non-browser fetch attempt** (default UA, browser UA, and Googlebot UA all blocked) and has no server-rendered HTML at all (`<app-root></app-root>` with JS bundles only) — the literal ToS clause text could not be retrieved for these two without a full headless/real browser session, which is itself the finding: **the site's own infrastructure is actively hostile to non-browser automated access**, independent of what the words in the ToS say.

## Headline finding

Of the 12 sources, **only GSA Auctions is unambiguously GREEN** (it publishes an official, free, public API and is bound by federal open-data law). **8 of the remaining 11 are RED** — several with clauses that describe almost exactly the product being planned here (a scraped closing-price comps database), naming "pricing tools," "dashboards," and "databases" explicitly. **3 are AMBER** (silent, not permission). Per the operating rule for this project, **AMBER/RED sources do not get built until Abdel signs off** — that is 11 of 12 non-GSA sources.

This also lines up with GovAuctions.app's own public posture: per the known facts, its `/listings` (live, fact-only) endpoint is scoped to **"sanctioned sources only, currently gsa"** — i.e., the operator who has already scaled this exact business to 29 sources and 150k+ comps still won't expose a live per-listing feed for anything but the one federally-mandated-open source. That is independent corroboration, not just our own reading of the clauses.

## Notable cross-source patterns

1. **Corporate siblings share a posture.** GovDeals and Bid4Assets are both owned by **Liquidity Services, Inc. (Nasdaq: LQDT)** — confirmed via the shared "Liquidity Services Brands" navigation bar rendered on Bid4Assets' own homepage (alongside AllSurplus, Machinio, Liquidation.com) and corroborated by Liquidity Services' own segment description (GovDeals segment "offers solutions through its GovDeals, Bid4Assets and Sierra marketplaces"). Both run identical Akamai-hostile infrastructure that blocks every non-browser fetch attempted in this pass. GovPlanet is part of **RB Global / Ritchie Bros**, whose Site Usage Terms are the single most explicit clause found (naming "pricing tools" and "dashboards" directly).
2. **"Robots.txt says nothing" is explicitly not a defense.** HiBid's ToS separately bans bypassing "robot exclusion headers," and several sites (Purple Wave, BidSpotter) mark their own semi-public API/search endpoints as non-crawlable in robots.txt while their ToS separately bans automated access outright — closing both doors at once.
3. **GSA is the only source with a legal *obligation*, not just a permission, to be open.** As a federal agency, GSA is bound by the OPEN Government Data Act (44 U.S.C. §3502h et seq., enacted via the Foundations for Evidence-Based Policymaking Act of 2018), which requires federal data assets to be published in open, machine-readable, non-proprietary formats "with no restrictions on copying, publishing, distributing, transmitting, citing, or adapting," reinforced by the pre-existing statutory Data.gov mandate. State sites (MiBid, iBid Illinois) are governed by weaker state-level open-records law (FOIA-equivalents) that address *records requests*, not live-site scraping.
4. **Two sites (GovDeals, Bid4Assets) could not be fully verified** because their ToS pages are unreadable outside a real browser (Akamai + client-only Angular SPA, zero server-rendered text, zero Wayback Machine snapshots found for any `*terms*` path). The RED verdict for these two rests on: confirmed site-wide bot-blocking infrastructure, absence of any sanctioned channel, and (for GovDeals) the peer-product signal that GovAuctions.app itself won't treat GovDeals as sanctioned. If Abdel wants a fully-quoted clause before deciding, the only way to get it is to load the page in a real logged-in browser session and copy the rendered text — flagged as an open question below.

## Open questions

1. GovDeals' and Bid4Assets' literal ToS text could not be retrieved by any non-browser method — worth a 5-minute manual check (open the page in the existing Playwright/Patchright session, read the rendered terms) before treating the RED verdict as fully evidence-based rather than inferred.
2. GovAuctions.app's `/comps` endpoint claims "full-market" P25/median/P75 coverage while `/listings` is GSA-only — implying its comps aggregate likely draws on a broader (possibly RED-rated) footprint than its live-listings feed. Worth understanding whether aggregate/statistical comps (no per-listing images, links, or raw records) carry different ToS/legal exposure than a live listings mirror — this could change the shape of what BLACKWHOLE builds first.
3. MiBid's robots.txt technically green-lights generic-UA "reference" crawling even though it name-blocks AI bots — is a comps database "reference use" or does it cross into something the state would object to? Only Michigan DTMB can answer this; worth a direct email before writing a scraper.
4. Several ToS documents (HiBid, BidSpotter, Purple Wave, GovPlanet) are close cousins in structure — is this coincidence, a shared law firm/template, or shared platform vendor? Not confirmed in this pass; if a licensing conversation is ever pursued, worth checking whether one agreement could cover multiple sources.
5. Should BLACKWHOLE pursue a formal "prior written permission" ask with any of the RED sources given the existing internal scraping already in production for single-lot sourcing (a materially smaller, non-republishing use)? That precedent doesn't extend automatically to a public/paid comps product and is worth Abdel's explicit read before any RED/AMBER source gets built.
